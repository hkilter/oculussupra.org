Signal S-010 · 23 November 2026 · event 5 November 2026 · 4 min

The Public Report Said Less Than 2. The Reviewer Computed 1.97.

[CH] primaryAutonomy level 0 · Reviewed in full4 min

Curated by H. Kemal İlter, written with Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2. Conflict declared: Anthropic, whose Risk Report is under review in this Signal, makes Claude, the model this Signal was written with.

Evidence

On 5 November 2026, METR published its review of the automated-R&D section of Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report, written at Anthropic's request with access to a private version of the report B2 (METR, 2026b).

The public report rates the risk "Low", says its leading indicators point to acceleration "by less than a factor of 2", and calls a doubling of the pace of progress attributable to AI R&D automation "a potential early warning" B1 (Anthropic, 2026; METR, 2026b). The private report's figures imply a rate of 1.97 by METR's own calculation, and METR suggests "around 2"; its own analysis finds reasonable estimates between 1.3 and 2.8 B2 (METR, 2026b). The private report, as METR quotes it, says "it seems plausible we do exceed this factor of 2 overall"; the public report's summary says "not yet by a factor of 2", with a caveat that measurement is difficult B2 (Anthropic, 2026; METR, 2026b).

METR agrees that catastrophic risk was low but finds it plausible that the early-warning threshold had been crossed by 15 July; most relevant researchers at METR and Anthropic lean the other way B2 (METR, 2026b). It says the public report "overall understates the level of acceleration", and offers replacement wording for three passages B2 (METR, 2026b).

A companion assessment estimates, with high uncertainty, that by October 2026 Anthropic was likely improving its models on well-specified tasks at around twice its earlier rate, and guesses that a majority of that acceleration, "perhaps ≈80%", is due to AI B2 (METR, 2026a). From that assessment Anthropic redacted "a majority of the evidence we found most informative", to remove, it said, confidential information that could accelerate other AI developers; the public, METR writes, has to rely on METR's judgment "much more than would be ideal" B2 (METR, 2026a).

InferenceSame evidence, read twice

The Integration reading

Read as Integration, a developer opened its private figures to an outside examiner, and the examiner's disagreement was published. Anthropic asked for the review; METR read the private report and said in public that the public one understates the acceleration (METR, 2026b). On the larger question the two agree: catastrophic risk was low, and most relevant researchers at METR and Anthropic lean toward the threshold not having been crossed (METR, 2026b). What remains is a dispute over the threshold, and the reviewer supplied its preferred language. The redactions have a stated purpose, and the reviewer's highest-level conclusions were published all the same (METR, 2026a). The weight rests on B2: the review was requested, written and published.

The Drift reading

Read as Drift, this is a line drawn to give early warning, and a public sentence that kept the measurement below it. The phrase "less than a factor of 2" stood for figures METR computes at 1.97, and the private report found exceeding the factor plausible (METR, 2026b). By October, METR estimates a rate likely around twice the earlier one, most of the acceleration guessed to be due to AI (METR, 2026a). A majority of the evidence METR found most informative is redacted, so the public relies on METR's judgment "much more than would be ideal" (METR, 2026a). The weight rests on B2: 1.97, published as less than 2.

Ruling: Drift, narrowly. The examination worked; what it found is a warning line that sat nearer in the private text than in the public one.

Text saying only that acceleration remains below a factor of 2, with or without a note of uncertainty, does not count. If no such text can be found on that date, the claim resolves as 0.
